# Voice and copy for decks

Voice is constant; tone dials by surface. The brand is the accountable
integrator: plainspoken, precise, grounded, industrial, accountable. Write to
one engineer, not "manufacturers".

## The six voice principles applied to slides

1. Lead with the claim, then prove it. The headline is the claim; the slide
   body is the proof. Never a topic label.
2. Specifics replace adjectives. "Tray sealing at up to 120 ppm, changeover
   under 20 minutes" beats any adjective. If you have no specific, say less.
3. Short sentences, point first. A 28-word windup that says nothing testable
   is the measured defect this brand exists to correct.
4. Partners are proof, not decoration. Name what the partner does on the
   line. A logo wall with no claims is banned by the brief.
5. Write to one reader. "You tell us the product and the rate. We spec the
   line that hits it."
6. Honest about limits. "No line runs at 100 percent. Here is our measured
   uptime and how fast we respond when it drops."

## Headline rules

- Sentence case. 12 words max. A complete, opinionated sentence.
- The chain test: read only the headlines in order; a reader should be able
  to make the decision without seeing a single body. If any headline is a
  label ("Market overview"), rewrite it as the claim the slide proves.
- Wrong: "Service capabilities". Right: "Parts same day, a technician on
  site within 4 hours."

## Mechanics (apply without exception)

- Sentence case for headings, buttons, labels. Title case only for proper
  nouns and product names.
- Contractions yes; full forms only in legal lines.
- Oxford comma always.
- Numerals for every measurable quantity, including under ten. One space
  between value and unit: 12 mm, 120 ppm, 4 hr.
- Percent: "15%" in tables, stats, and UI; "15 percent" in running prose.
- Ranges use "to": 5 to 8 mm, WK 2 to 6. Never a dash. No em-dashes
  anywhere in any copy.
- Tolerances and dimensions: ±0.1 mm, 300 x 200 mm.
- Part numbers, spec values, dates, and stats always in JetBrains Mono.
- "We" is Harpak-ULMA; "you" is the customer. No third-person
  self-reference outside boilerplate.

## Banned and watch list

Hard ban (never ship, including in speaker notes): seamless, unlock,
AI-powered, synergy, leverage, best-in-class, revolutionary, cutting-edge,
world-class, game-changing, state-of-the-art, turnkey (unless literally
turnkey and defined).

Watch list (replace with a specific): solutions (name the machine or line),
end-to-end (say "product to pallet"), drive innovation, redefining the
standards, minimize waste, maximize efficiency, user-friendly.

Say this, not that:

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| end-to-end solutions | a complete line, product to pallet |
| best-in-class partners | category specialists we integrate and stand behind |
| our solutions | the tray sealer, the thermoforming line |
| seamless integration | we wire it into the controls and line you already run |
| world-class service | parts same day, a technician on site within 4 hours [GET] |
| maximize efficiency | cut changeover from 30 to 18 minutes [GET] |
| AI-powered insights | Aura reads line data and flags a fault before the line stops |
| powered by next-gen technology | name it: FactoryTalk, Mondini tray sealing |

## Tone by deck type

| Deck | Tone | Example register |
|---|---|---|
| Customer pitch / line assessment | Confident and plain; one claim per slide | "We build your line, product to pallet." |
| Technical / solution deck | Dense and exact; specs lead | "Thermoformer: up to 120 mm draw depth, Allen-Bradley control." [GET] |
| Webinar / thought leadership | Educational, evidence-first; the one thesis is the accountable integrator | Sourced quotes, measured numbers, one argument |
| Service content | Direct, commitments in time and parts | "Parts shipped same day." [GET] |
| Event / trade show | One claim per slide, big type, minimal body | "One line. One number to call." |
| Aura content | Action-first, terse; no marketing voice inside product surfaces | "Seal temperature dropped on Lane 2. Check tooling." |

## Audiences (write the deck to the room)

| Audience | They care about | So the deck leads with |
|---|---|---|
| Plant and process engineers | Throughput, changeover, tolerances, integration, maintainability | Specs and integration proof, not benefit copy |
| Operations leaders | Uptime, OEE, total cost, risk, service response | Who owns uptime, commitments in numbers |
| Procurement | TCO, contract scope, single accountability, lead times | The one-contract one-owner model, stated plainly |
| Food safety and quality | Sanitary design, compliance, traceability | Hygiene and validation proof, not buried |

## Evidence rule (hardest rule in the book)

Every performance figure cites a source or is labeled a target/illustrative
with a [GET] tag. A figure with no source does not ship. Customer quotes must
be real and approved; attribute by role on the line, not adjectives. Claims
list needing sign-off before use: exclusivity or market-position claims
beyond "the exclusive North American source for G. Mondini tray sealing"
(that one is approved).
